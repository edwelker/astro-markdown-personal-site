These are my personal variations on two classic Fannie Farmer cranberry preparations, kept as two separate processes: a **10-minute boiled sauce** with fresh ginger and freshly ground cardamom, and a **no-cook food-processed orange relish** with fresh chili heat.

---

### Process 1: Boiled Ginger-Cardamom Sauce

Adapted from Fannie Merritt Farmer, *The Boston Cooking-School Cook Book* (Boston: Little, Brown, and Company, 1896; public domain via [Project Gutenberg #65061](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/65061) / [Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/bostoncookingsch00farm_0)):

> *"Pick over and wash three cups cranberries. Put in a stewpan, add one and one-fourth cups sugar and one cup boiling water. Cover, and boil ten minutes. Care must be taken that they do not boil over. Skim and cool."*

* **My Tweaks:** I use the low end for sugar (`225g` instead of `250g`) and freshly grind cardamom seeds in a spice grinder (`3-4` fresh green pods, or `~5` older pantry pods) alongside grated fresh ginger.
* **Optional Upgrades:** Bloom thin ginger coins, cracked green cardamom pods, and a wide strip of orange peel in the boiling liquid for `3 min` before adding berries; swap half the water (`120 mL`) for apple cider or fresh orange juice; finish off-heat with `1/8 tsp` fine iodized salt.

### Process 2: Chili-Citrus Raw Cranberry-Orange Relish

Adapted from later editions of *The Fannie Farmer Cookbook* and modernized for a 3-stage Cuisinart food processor pulse.

*(Draft note before publishing: confirm the exact revised Fannie Farmer Cookbook edition for the uncooked cranberry-orange relish against your physical copy before setting `draft: false`.)*

* **My Tweaks:** I use the low end for sugar (`150g / ¾ cup`), add **`2/3` of a medium Fresno chili** (or `1/2-1` jalapeño), and hold back `¼` of the cranberries for `2-3` light finishing pulses so the relish keeps bright, intact berry pops instead of turning into slush.
* **Optional Upgrades:** Add `1/4 tsp` fine salt and `1 tbsp` fresh lime juice or apple cider vinegar during Stage 1 to speed up maceration and keep the chili bright against rich gravy.
